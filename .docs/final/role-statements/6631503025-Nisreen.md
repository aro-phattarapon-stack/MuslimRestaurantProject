## Reflection

### 1. Lessons Learned
Building an application with AI has changed how software engineering works across requirements, design, coding, testing, quality, security, and teamwork. AI helped our team generate code, explore design ideas, and organize requirements faster. However, I learned that AI-generated results are not always accurate and still require strict human review and validation.

In my project, one thing that got better was development and preparation efficiency. AI helped our team speed up prototyping and reduced test case preparation time by 50%. However, one thing that got worse was quality and scope alignment. AI frequently generated test cases for unbuilt Phase 2 features, and even when the app looked functional, it still contained usability issues and privacy risks (such as student IDs in raw survey exports). This made real-user testing and data anonymization essential—software quality must be evaluated based on actual user experiences, not just whether code runs.

### 2. Where I Stand in the Software Industry Today (in the AI Era)
I believe AI is becoming an essential tool in the software industry. Software professionals should not compete with AI, but learn how to work effectively alongside it. I see myself growing into a **Software Quality Assurance (QA) and Software Testing** role. Human judgment, critical thinking, privacy awareness, and understanding real user needs will remain irreplaceable, even as AI becomes more capable.

### 3. My Plan: Next Year and in 5 Years
**Next year**, I plan to improve my software testing skills—especially test automation (Playwright, Vitest), API testing, and security testing—and gain practical experience on real-world software projects.

**In 5 years**, I aim to become an experienced **QA Engineer** or **Software Test Engineer** who designs testing strategies, improves product quality, and uses AI responsibly to boost productivity while maintaining professional judgment.
